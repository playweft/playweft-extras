# Bubble Kick · 泡泡球场

An original browser recreation of QQ Tang's bomb-kicking football loop. The Canvas 2D renderer combines locally bundled generated raster artwork with procedural effects; it uses no extracted original game assets or remote resources.

Open `/bubble-kick/` for a four-character practice match (one human + three bots) or same-keyboard two-player mode (one bot teammate each). Playweft solo embedding also supports practice. Room mode supports 2–4 humans, alternating teams, and spectators; use two or four players for balanced teams. No bots fill room seats.

- P1: WASD / arrows. Local P2: arrows, P1 uses WASD. Balls are kicked by aligned physical contact while moving into them.
- Items: P1 uses keys 1–7 or the seven inventory slots. Local P2 uses numpad 1–7, with inventory shown in their roster entry. Touch uses a left joystick and three right-hand item buttons (glue, banana, fork); same-screen P2 requires a keyboard.
- Item buttons activate on pointer down, including a second finger while the joystick remains held. Browser compatibility clicks are ignored after pointer activation; keyboard and accessibility activation remain available.
- System waves throw bubbles into the arena from above. Normal yellow bubbles blast one cell in each cardinal direction; green football bubbles blast four. Kicks aim eight cells along the kick ray, using the original 15 × 13 map coordinates (lawn offset 1,4). If that target is a wall, goal or terrace, test shorter distances 7 down to 1, keeping the kick on the same ray. Horizontal and vertical kicks both start at eight cells; different blocked cells and map dimensions can shorten their effective distances. Airborne bubbles do not block players. Their fuse keeps counting; natural expiry waits for landing, but another explosion can chain-trigger them at their registered destination.
- Overlapping bubbles raise and draw the earliest-created football on top; if there is no football, they use the earliest-created bubble. Each cell selects only one representative. Its two-pixel visual lift eases out over 100 ms. An incoming kick blends directly to the raised landing endpoint, without an extra rise after landing. Shadows, collision, explosion cells and server state keep their original positions.
- Bubbles can land on occupied cells; all grounded bubbles sharing a cell are unkickable. Their explosions chain, with each arm stopping at the next bubble, which then contributes its own range. Foam remains visible for 0.5 s, but the hit test occurs only on impact.
- A ball landing under a character does not pin them: already overlapping bodies can walk out in any direction. The native three-pixel entry strip still prevents walking into grounded bubbles from outside. Glue and banana can be placed in the current cell while moving, without releasing direction or the touch joystick.
- Blast exposure traps a player for 4 s. A teammate within 0.75 cell of their ground centre rescues them with 1.2 s immunity; opponents within the same radius or expiry eliminate them. Contact uses Euclidean distance in continuous grid coordinates, including across cell boundaries; simultaneous enemy contact takes priority over rescue. All exposure resolves before rescue.
- Whole-batch drops begin at approximately scene-clock second 5. Every 20 ms the scheduler checks whether the time since the previous batch reaches `max(4000, 9000 - floor(elapsedMs / 8))` milliseconds. It does not lock in the interval at the previous batch. The opening countdown is included in the scene clock; pauses freeze it, resuming preserves it, and a rematch resets it.
- Each type’s native batch count is multiplied by 1.5 and rounded up throughout the round, at the user’s request to increase density: `ceil(1.5 * min(15, 5 + floor(elapsedMs / 16000)))` normal bubbles and `ceil(1.5 * min(10, 1 + floor(elapsedMs / 16000)))` football bubbles. The opening batch is 8 normal + 2 football (previously 5 + 1); counts increase every 16 seconds to 23 + 15 (previously 15 + 10). Cells are sampled without replacement within a batch over the whole field, including cells occupied by older bubbles.
- Every bomb wave also throws one collectible while stock remains: shoes (weight 5, stock 50), banana (2, 50), glue (2, 50), fork (1, 4). Coins are deliberately excluded at the user's request; the remaining weights renormalize. Shoes immediately raise speed, up to three levels. Glue grants three placements; banana and fork grant one use. Ground glue slows for 10 s; banana clears slow and forces sliding in the entry direction until blocked. A placed trap spares players already overlapping it until they leave, and can affect its owner on re-entry. Forks self-rescue a trapped player. There are no periodic shield drops.
- Bombs destroy pickups and placed hazards. The recycling queue dispatches on a 30 s gate, retaining collectible versus placed-hazard type. Its fall animation is currently the same 0.8 s approximation as wave items.
- Eliminate the other team to win. A four-minute scene-clock timeout (including Ready/Go) or simultaneous elimination is a draw. Scores persist across rematches in the current session.

Cadence and count rules live in `bomb-rules.js`, mirrored in Lua. The native cadence and underlying counts, before the deliberate 50% density increase, come from the [pinned reverse-engineering implementation](https://github.com/kuuhaku1314/qqtang/blob/676ac02c6d512ab27bc0f5df8a9ebff8b8c06c70/internal/server/probe/competitive_airborne.go#L31-L67), which attributes the threshold to native `FUN_005e17c5`. Continuous threshold evaluation is inferred from two football02 rounds in [this 2021 original recording](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1Ji4y1P7ZE/?t=73), rather than copying the repository's separate Boss timer. With 20 ms checks, wave dispatch times relative to the beginning of the scene clock are 5.00, 12.46, 19.08, 24.96, 30.20, 34.86, 39.00, 43.00, 47.00 seconds, then every 4 seconds. The two recording samples match that progression within roughly 0.15 seconds after aligning their initial epochs. The recording shows Ready/Go while the four-minute timer already counts down. The approximate 5-second first dispatch plus the current 0.8-second fall agrees with the observed first arrival; the producer's initial epoch remains incompletely verified.

Contact-kick flight is 0.4 s. The original 3 s fuse becomes due only at negative remaining time: this version fires on the first 20 ms step after 3 s (3.02 s), retaining its deadline across kicks. Fall duration (0.8 s), RNG and exact target distribution remain approximations. One/four-cell ranges and immovable stacks are recorded in the [2015 player guide](https://jingyan.baidu.com/article/da1091fb307e80027849d680.html). Eight-cell kicks, 15 × 13 wrapping and backward terrain fallback are checked against the football contact producer `0060cf49`, specifically `0060d0e7–0060d13f`; the separate throw producer `006192ba` is not used as football-kick evidence. Giant bubbles are not implemented.


`game.js` simulates motion every 20 ms, at 5.1 cells/s before items (shoe levels: 5.8, 6.7, 8; slow: 1.8; slide: 13), with 100 ms logical timers and hazard-aware bots. `movement.js` sweeps a player body continuously using fixed-point geometry. The movement footprint is 0.95 cell wide (native ±19 pixels on a 40-pixel cell). Partial obstructions (up to 0.475 cell overlap), including goal corners, induce perpendicular clearance at 4 cells/s before forward motion resumes. Goal clearance uses the nearest actual rectangle edge; full goal faces remain solid and forced banana slides stop at contact. Full obstructions stop the player. A 22-pixel forward probe (0.55 cell) handles contact kicks before movement. Explosions and pickups use the nearest cell centre without snapping the player. Rescue and enemy contact instead use a 0.75-cell radius, evaluated every motion step in the same fixed-point coordinates in JavaScript and Lua. `game.lua` implements the authoritative human room rules. Input messages are complete held-state snapshots at 10 Hz with round and sequence checks. Only server time drives room simulation; silent clients lose held inputs after 400 ms, rooms pause after a two-second silence, and stalled rooms restart with a countdown rather than lethal catch-up. Spectators cannot advance the simulation. A departing player closes the match. No progress is persisted.

The manifest declares `orientation: "landscape"`; Playweft owns direction locking and compatibility display. Standalone portrait screens show a rotation gate and suspend local input/simulation.

The landscape HUD uses equal-width side rails, keeping the arena centred in the entire viewport. Time, scores and match controls sit on the left; players and inventory sit on the right. The arena scales uniformly to the available space and displays source y=110–800, shortening the crowd terrace while retaining the whole lawn and the first-row kick apex. The joystick uses a lower-left activation region: touch/pen down establishes a neutral input origin, dragging steers relative to that origin, and release/cancellation stops movement. Its artwork is clamped outside the playable lawn and clear of match controls; the idle hint is inset from the screen edges and does not take part in the information layout. Both touch control groups float over the side areas, clear of the playable lawn. All touch layouts use an icon-only cluster with a larger glue button below two smaller banana/self-rescue buttons and hide the duplicate numbered inventory panel. The right cluster is a fixed overlay outside the sidebar layout, inset from the right by `clamp(38px, 6vw, 80px)` and from the bottom by `clamp(40px, 12vh, 72px)`, also respecting notch/home-indicator safe areas. Compact roster cards leave room above the cluster on short screens; keyboard shortcuts remain available.

The 13 × 9 simulation grid matches the lawn inside the original football02 cone barriers. Each goal occupies the outer column, rows 3–5; upper rows 0–2 and lower rows 6–8 remain walkable. Goal bodies block characters, blast propagation, falling targets and kick landing targets. The outer player centres reserve space for the full character sprite and trapped bubble so neither overlaps the cones. White pitch lines are markings, not movement boundaries. This follows the side-lane movement visible in [the original football02 recording](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1Ji4y1P7ZE/?t=80). Players, bubbles, pickups and blast tiles share this wider ground projection in both local and room play; the left/right pitch markings remain inset by one outer column, while the top/bottom markings meet the outer row edges.

`art.js` loads the generated stadium and transparent 4 × 4 character atlas from `public/bubble-kick/art/`. Rows face down, left, right and up; columns form the walking cycle. Crops in `hero-walk.json` contain two manually authored foot support centres per frame. Their midpoint, rather than the lowest alpha pixel or shoe tip, is registered to the ground position in all sixteen frames. This moves the visible body roughly 8–11 pixels down compared with the former bottom-edge anchor, while movement, collision, depth sorting and shadows retain the same ground point. HUD portraits explicitly retain their bottom anchor to fit their small canvases. Team recolouring preserves skin and cream trim. `prompts.json` records the built-in ImageGen prompts and revisions. Assets resolve relative to the document, including nested deployment paths.

Horizontal movement uses the separate `hero-walk-side.png` atlas (left/right rows, four poses each). The original side poses repeatedly led with the same near leg; the replacement swaps the near/far legs and opposing arm swing in the second half of the cycle. Its `hero-walk-side.json` registers the same midpoint of the two feet, and records the feet in near/far order. Front/back poses, the 115 ms frame interval and movement rules are unchanged. Both atlases receive the same four team palettes; the original side frames remain a loading fallback.

`public/bubble-kick/icon.svg` is a hand-redrawn vector mascot for the wider bubble-arena game, without soccer-specific symbols. It uses paths, basic shapes and editable gradients, with a square blue background lightest at the centre. The generated design reference and its prompt are preserved in `art/icon-concept.png` and `art/icon-concept.prompt.txt`.

`render.js` uses a 960 × 800 arena inside the adaptive outer viewport. Alternating vertical mowing bands follow the 13 lawn columns, composited over the grass texture before white markings and actors. Grounded bubbles register the centre of their lower footprint to the cell centre, with their silhouette extending five pixels in front of it; the previous lowest-edge anchor made them appear too high. This five-pixel depth is visual tuning shared by normal and football bubbles and scales with their pulse; the ground pivot stays stable through pulse, fall landing and kick flight. Shared ground shadows are centred on their actual ground anchors, with no extra two-pixel downward offset. It draws separate soft ground shadows, connected foam blast tiles and a shared ground-depth order for players, pickups and bubbles. Positions interpolate linearly between continuous simulation samples; there is no exponential easing. Room poses use a 100 ms interpolation buffer. `touch-controls.js` handles captured joystick input. `main.js` handles keyboard, focus cleanup, optional synthesized sound, bridge input and round UI. `/help.html` documents controls and rules.

The first ground row is centred half of the actual 60-pixel row height below the lawn's y=225 edge (y=255). Full explosion tiles begin at that edge; their rim strokes stay inside outer cells. Actors, bubbles, items, goals and all pitch markings use this same projection, without clipping away part of the first row. The top and bottom sidelines are y=225/765, exactly at the full blast rows’ outer edges; penalty boxes use whole-cell dimensions. Mirrored goal frames have equal four-pixel outlines, red on the left and green on the right, followed by one-pixel nets.

Run `npm run check` for the package build and regression suite. Room-rule tests use the same Lua source as production and verify parity against local rules for seeded human inputs.

Local bots use the original roaming behaviour: every 200 ms, score adjacent cells with random variation, open exits, imminent explosions and nearby trapped players. They keep moving between decisions and stop at their actual continuous position. Every 20 ms, a separate movement guard prevents entering still-visible explosion foam, including a held direction selected before the explosion and perpendicular edge assistance. Bots already inside foam can leave. This guard affects bot steering only; human movement and the impact-only damage rule are unchanged. Regression tests cover roaming, fresh-explosion entry, the last animation frames, and movement resuming after the foam clears.

For local room integration, run `npm run dev:bubble-kick` and open `/__dev/?seat=1` and `/__dev/?seat=2` in two windows. Use `PORT=9142 npm run dev:bubble-kick` to avoid other servers. The development-only host is excluded from production.

Original-rule evidence and scope are recorded in [original-rules.md](original-rules.md). The implementation uses derived numerical rules only; it does not bundle the original executable or map resources. Inventory actions carry their own increasing sequence and retry until the room snapshot acknowledges them, independently of held movement input.
