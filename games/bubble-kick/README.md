# Bubble Kick · 泡泡球场

An original browser recreation of QQ Tang's bomb-kicking football loop. The Canvas 2D renderer combines locally bundled generated raster artwork with procedural effects; it uses no extracted original game assets or remote resources.

Open `/bubble-kick/` for a four-character practice match (one human + three bots) or same-keyboard two-player mode (one bot teammate each). Playweft solo embedding also supports practice. Room mode supports 2–4 humans, alternating teams, and spectators; use two or four players for balanced teams. No bots fill room seats.

- P1: WASD / arrows. Local P2: arrows, P1 uses WASD. Balls are kicked by aligned physical contact while moving into them.
- Items: P1 uses keys 1–7 or the seven inventory slots. Local P2 uses numpad 1–7, with inventory shown in their roster entry. Touch uses a left joystick and three right-hand item buttons (glue, banana, fork); same-screen P2 requires a keyboard.
- System waves throw bubbles into the arena from above. Normal yellow bubbles blast one cell in each cardinal direction; green football bubbles blast four. Kicks aim eight cells along the kick ray, using the original 15 × 13 map coordinates (lawn offset 1,4). If that target is a wall, goal or terrace, test shorter distances 7 down to 1, keeping the kick on the same ray. Horizontal and vertical kicks both start at eight cells; different blocked cells and map dimensions can shorten their effective distances. Airborne bubbles do not block players. Their fuse keeps counting; natural expiry waits for landing, but another explosion can chain-trigger them at their registered destination.
- Bubbles can land on occupied cells; all grounded bubbles sharing a cell are unkickable. Their explosions chain, with each arm stopping at the next bubble, which then contributes its own range. Foam remains visible for 0.5 s, but the hit test occurs only on impact.
- Blast exposure traps a player for 4 s. A teammate within 0.75 cell of their ground centre rescues them with 1.2 s immunity; opponents within the same radius or expiry eliminate them. Contact uses Euclidean distance in continuous grid coordinates, including across cell boundaries; simultaneous enemy contact takes priority over rescue. All exposure resolves before rescue.
- Whole-batch drops begin at approximately scene-clock second 5. Every 20 ms the scheduler checks whether the time since the previous batch reaches `max(4000, 9000 - floor(elapsedMs / 8))` milliseconds. It does not lock in the interval at the previous batch. The opening countdown is included in the scene clock; pauses freeze it, resuming preserves it, and a rematch resets it.
- Each batch contains `min(15, 5 + floor(elapsedMs / 16000))` normal bubbles and `min(10, 1 + floor(elapsedMs / 16000))` football bubbles. Both counts rise every 16 seconds, independently capped at 15 and 10. Cells are sampled without replacement within a batch over the whole field, including cells occupied by older bubbles.
- Every bomb wave also throws one collectible while stock remains: shoes (weight 5, stock 50), banana (2, 50), glue (2, 50), fork (1, 4). Coins are deliberately excluded at the user's request; the remaining weights renormalize. Shoes immediately raise speed, up to three levels. Glue grants three placements; banana and fork grant one use. Ground glue slows for 10 s; banana clears slow and forces sliding in the entry direction until blocked. A placed trap spares players already overlapping it until they leave, and can affect its owner on re-entry. Forks self-rescue a trapped player. There are no periodic shield drops.
- Bombs destroy pickups and placed hazards. The recycling queue dispatches on a 30 s gate, retaining collectible versus placed-hazard type. Its fall animation is currently the same 0.8 s approximation as wave items.
- Eliminate the other team to win. A four-minute scene-clock timeout (including Ready/Go) or simultaneous elimination is a draw. Scores persist across rematches in the current session.

Cadence and count rules live in `bomb-rules.js`, mirrored in Lua. Their source is the [pinned reverse-engineering implementation](https://github.com/kuuhaku1314/qqtang/blob/676ac02c6d512ab27bc0f5df8a9ebff8b8c06c70/internal/server/probe/competitive_airborne.go#L31-L67), which attributes the threshold to native `FUN_005e17c5`. Continuous threshold evaluation is inferred from two football02 rounds in [this 2021 original recording](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1Ji4y1P7ZE/?t=73), rather than copying the repository's separate Boss timer. With 20 ms checks, wave dispatch times relative to the beginning of the scene clock are 5.00, 12.46, 19.08, 24.96, 30.20, 34.86, 39.00, 43.00, 47.00 seconds, then every 4 seconds. The two recording samples match that progression within roughly 0.15 seconds after aligning their initial epochs. The recording shows Ready/Go while the four-minute timer already counts down. The approximate 5-second first dispatch plus the current 0.8-second fall agrees with the observed first arrival; the producer's initial epoch remains incompletely verified.

Contact-kick flight is 0.4 s. The original 3 s fuse becomes due only at negative remaining time: this version fires on the first 20 ms step after 3 s (3.02 s), retaining its deadline across kicks. Fall duration (0.8 s), RNG and exact target distribution remain approximations. One/four-cell ranges and immovable stacks are recorded in the [2015 player guide](https://jingyan.baidu.com/article/da1091fb307e80027849d680.html). Eight-cell kicks, 15 × 13 wrapping and backward terrain fallback are checked against the football contact producer `0060cf49`, specifically `0060d0e7–0060d13f`; the separate throw producer `006192ba` is not used as football-kick evidence. Giant bubbles are not implemented.


`game.js` simulates motion every 20 ms, at 5.1 cells/s before items (shoe levels: 5.8, 6.7, 8; slow: 1.8; slide: 13), with 100 ms logical timers and hazard-aware bots. `movement.js` sweeps a player body continuously using fixed-point geometry. The movement footprint is 0.95 cell wide (native ±19 pixels on a 40-pixel cell). Partial obstructions (up to 0.475 cell overlap) induce perpendicular clearance at 4 cells/s before forward motion resumes. Full obstructions stop the player. A 22-pixel forward probe (0.55 cell) handles contact kicks before movement. Explosions and pickups use the nearest cell centre without snapping the player. Rescue and enemy contact instead use a 0.75-cell radius, evaluated every motion step in the same fixed-point coordinates in JavaScript and Lua. `game.lua` implements the authoritative human room rules. Input messages are complete held-state snapshots at 10 Hz with round and sequence checks. Only server time drives room simulation; silent clients lose held inputs after 400 ms, rooms pause after a two-second silence, and stalled rooms restart with a countdown rather than lethal catch-up. Spectators cannot advance the simulation. A departing player closes the match. No progress is persisted.

The manifest declares `orientation: "landscape"`; Playweft owns direction locking and compatibility display. Standalone portrait screens show a rotation gate and suspend local input/simulation.

The game uses a fixed 1024 × 768 (4:3) logical viewport. The field and HUD scale together to fit the window, centered with black letterboxing; resizing does not rearrange the UI. Touch controls float above the viewport at a fixed usable size, including in the black margins; they are shown for coarse pointers and touch/pen input.

The 13 × 9 simulation grid matches the lawn inside the original football02 cone barriers. Each goal occupies the outer column, rows 3–5; upper rows 0–2 and lower rows 6–8 remain walkable. Goal bodies block characters, blast propagation, falling targets and kick landing targets. The outer player centres reserve space for the full character sprite and trapped bubble so neither overlaps the cones. White pitch lines are markings, not movement boundaries. This follows the side-lane movement visible in [the original football02 recording](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1Ji4y1P7ZE/?t=80). Players, bubbles, pickups and blast tiles share this wider ground projection in both local and room play; the pitch markings keep their inset position.

`art.js` loads the generated stadium and transparent 4 × 4 character atlas from `public/bubble-kick/art/`. Rows face down, left, right and up; columns form the walking cycle. Alpha bounds in `hero-walk.json` keep every frame anchored at the feet. Team recolouring preserves skin and cream trim. `prompts.json` records the built-in ImageGen prompts and revisions. Assets resolve relative to the document, including nested deployment paths.

`render.js` uses a 960 × 800 arena inside the fixed outer viewport. Alternating vertical mowing bands follow the 13 lawn columns, composited over the grass texture before white markings and actors. It draws separate soft ground shadows, connected foam blast tiles and a shared ground-depth order for players, pickups and bubbles. Positions interpolate linearly between continuous simulation samples; there is no exponential easing. Room poses use a 100 ms interpolation buffer. `touch-controls.js` handles captured joystick input. `main.js` handles keyboard, focus cleanup, optional synthesized sound, bridge input and round UI. `/help.html` documents controls and rules.

Run `npm run check` for the package build and regression suite. Room-rule tests use the same Lua source as production and verify parity against local rules for seeded human inputs.

Local bots plan routes against visible falling targets, bomb occupancy and predicted impact times, including blast shielding and airborne chains. They leave landing zones before being pinned, wait in safe refuges, avoid placed traps and probe real body movement around goal corners. If bubbles seal every safe walking route, they test contact kicks against the resulting landing and chain forecast, opening more space for a second kick when necessary. Decisions do not consume world RNG or reveal future waves. The survival target is at least 180 seconds (75% of the four-minute scene clock) without other players interfering. `npm run benchmark:bubble-kick-ai -- 1024 10001` runs reproducible scenarios, alternating all four starting seats. Only one actor is present; the harness ignores team-victory termination while retaining ordinary movement, items, bomb cadence and damage. First capture ends the measurement, even with a rescue fork. Regression tests cover 64 seeded scenarios, four late-wave sealed pockets, and physical/forecast edge cases.

For local room integration, run `npm run dev:bubble-kick` and open `/__dev/?seat=1` and `/__dev/?seat=2` in two windows. Use `PORT=9142 npm run dev:bubble-kick` to avoid other servers. The development-only host is excluded from production.

Original-rule evidence and scope are recorded in [original-rules.md](original-rules.md). The implementation uses derived numerical rules only; it does not bundle the original executable or map resources. Inventory actions carry their own increasing sequence and retry until the room snapshot acknowledges them, independently of held movement input.
